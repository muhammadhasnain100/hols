Final Patient Questionnaire Flow & Peptide Mapping
Provider-facing intake · v1.0 · July 2026
1. Purpose & Design Principles
This document defines the end-to-end questionnaire the chatbot runs with a provider who is seeking peptides for a patient. The bot collects structured answers, applies safety filters, then returns a ranked shortlist of the most relevant peptides with a reasoning trace. All output is a recommendation for the provider’s clinical judgment — never a prescription or instruction to use.
* Adaptive, not flat: a goal router opens only the relevant branch, so a typical patient answers ~15–18 questions, not 40.
* Safety-first: a red-flag gate runs before recommendations and can suppress an entire category (non-overridable).
* Evidence-honest: every peptide carries an evidence tier so FDA-approved options and early-stage research compounds never read the same.
* Traceable: each recommendation states which answers drove it, so the provider can audit the logic.
Flow Architecture
STAGE 0  Consent / framing
STAGE 1  Patient snapshot        (always — 5 Qs)
STAGE 2  Safety gate             (always — red-flag screen)
STAGE 3  Goal router             (1 Q — selects branch)
STAGE 4  Goal-specific deep dive (1 branch loads — 4–6 Qs)
STAGE 5  Meds / history / labs   (always)
STAGE 6  Preferences             (route, tolerance, prior response)
STAGE 7  OUTPUT: ranked peptides + reasoning + safety flags + labs

2. Stage 0 — Consent & Framing
Before any clinical question, the bot displays a one-time acknowledgement:
“This tool provides peptide recommendations to support your clinical decision-making. It does not prescribe, diagnose, or replace professional judgment. You confirm you are a licensed provider entering information about a patient.”
* Provider confirms: “I understand — continue.”

7. Stage 5 — History & Labs (always)
* Q12. Prior peptide/hormone therapy? Which, response, side effects — avoids re-suggesting failures and personalizes.
* Q13. Relevant labs on file? Surfaces the branch-specific baseline the bot will recommend obtaining before initiation.
Goal branch
	Recommended baseline labs
	Weight / metabolic (A)
	HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, lipid panel
	GH / vitality (C)
	IGF-1 (required), consider thyroid panel
	Immune (F)
	CBC, inflammatory markers (CRP)
	Sexual health (D)
	Blood pressure, testosterone (context)
	All
	Comprehensive metabolic panel, age-appropriate screening
	Labs are recommended, not blocking — the bot flags what to obtain rather than halting the flow.
8. Stage 6 — Preferences
* Q14. Injection tolerance — daily subcutaneous OK / prefer less frequent / prefer oral or topical. (Filters delivery route.)
* Q15. Complexity — single peptide vs. open to a validated stack.
* Q16. Timeline / commitment — short cycle vs. ongoing.
9. Stage 7 — Output
The recommendation card returns, in order:
* Ranked shortlist (2–4 peptides) for the primary goal — scored on goal-fit + evidence strength + safety.
* Reasoning trace per suggestion — which answers drove it.
* Suggested validated stack when a secondary goal or established combo applies (e.g., BPC-157 + TB-500; CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin).
* Safety flags fired — contraindications caught and monitoring notes (e.g., “confirm BP for PT-141,” “IGF-1 baseline needed”).
* Recommended baseline labs before initiation.
* Standing disclaimer — recommendation for provider judgment, not a prescription.
Ranking logic (composite score)
score = goalFit(0.5) + evidenceTier(0.3) + safetyFit(0.2)
Safety filters are applied first as hard gates; the composite score only ranks the surviving eligible peptides. A red flag can empty a category entirely — in which case the bot explains the exclusion instead of forcing a suggestion.

	11. Regulatory Note (2026)
On April 23, 2026 the FDA removed twelve peptides — including BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, Epitalon, Semax, DSIP and injectable GHK-Cu — from the Section 503A Category 2 list that had restricted compounding. Sermorelin, tesamorelin and PT-141 (bremelanotide) already have established prescription pathways. The Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) was scheduled to meet July 23–24, 2026 to discuss the 503A bulk-substance list. Availability and legal status shift — the bot should carry a current-status tag per peptide and defer to Frontier BioMed’s legal/regulatory guidance before finalizing any recommendation.
